# Dara

<Lede>
A creative-operations team at an agency or brand can generate thousands of assets across
model providers and still have no durable answer to basic operational questions. Dara makes
those answers part of the client-media supply chain instead of leaving them in spreadsheets,
chat history, and provider dashboards.
</Lede>

## The questions this answers

- **Attribution.** Which model produced this asset, with what parameters?
- **Cost.** What did it cost, including the seven attempts that were thrown away?
- **Reproducibility.** Can the original conditions be reconstructed, or do we start over?
- **Disclosure.** What can be handed to a client that does not also hand them the brief?
- **Control.** How would an expensive batch have been stopped *before* the invoice?

## What Dara is not

<Callout type="warning" title="Not an adversarial authenticity system">
Dara is tamper-evident **within an organisation that controls its own storage**. It is not a
defence against a motivated forger. C2PA or an external signer is the appropriate next layer,
and Dara says so rather than overclaiming. See the [trust model](/docs/trust-model).
</Callout>

Also explicitly out of scope: user accounts and roles, payments, a timeline or node editor,
model hosting or fine-tuning, and a mobile app.
